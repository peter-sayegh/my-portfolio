Publications

Levitation and Stability in Maglev Systems

P. A. Sayegh, H. Déo, Y. Abou Rabii, and A. Couairon. European Journal of Physics (2026).

We built a scaled-down maglev train from permanent-magnet strips and measured how its levitation height changes with load. We derived a closed-form model of the magnetic force from first principles that matches 24 measurements with a single fitted parameter (R2 = 0.96), explained its vertical stability through Earnshaw's theorem, and scaled the model up to the full-size Transrapid TR08.

SSCS Chipathon 2026: Photoplethysmography (PPG) Analog Front-End on GF180MCU

May – November 2026 · Report coming soon

Team tapeout project in the IEEE SSCS Chipathon, designing a PPG analog front-end for heart-rate sensing on the open-source GlobalFoundries GF180MCU PDK. The front-end chains a pulsed LED driver, a capacitive-feedback transimpedance amplifier built around a folded-cascode OTA, and active filtering with a fully differential amplifier. My blocks include a pulsed LED driver (triode NMOS current-set device plus a switch device, 8 mA peak) and a 25 µA / 5 µA NMOS bias current mirror laid out in a common-centroid, guard-ringed array (83.4 × 9.0 µm). Both layouts are DRC clean and LVS matched, and schematics and layouts for the full design are complete.

GPS Tracker Prototype: My First Hands-On Hardware Build

December 2024 – January 2025 · Early project

My first step from theory into hardware, built to learn how integrated circuits and embedded software come together on a real board. I paired an Adafruit Feather M0 (ATSAMD21 ARM Cortex-M0+ microcontroller) with an Ultimate GPS FeatherWing, which carries a MediaTek GPS receiver IC, powered from USB or a 3.7 V, 3000 mAh LiPo cell. In Arduino C++ I read the receiver's NMEA sentences over a serial (UART) link, parsed them with the Adafruit GPS library, and printed latitude, longitude, altitude and satellite count to the Serial Monitor once a valid fix was acquired. On an outdoor test the board reported a fix at 48°42.74′ N, 2°13.01′ E (171 m altitude, 3 satellites), matching my location at École Polytechnique in Palaiseau. I originally conceived it as a smart dog collar; adding a wireless link to a phone was left for future work. Along the way I learned to read IC datasheets, work with serial protocols and 3.3 V logic, and debug hardware outdoors, which is what pushed me toward circuit design.

Electrical Engineering Projects (Graduate)

Resonant Inductive Wireless Power Link: Pot-Core vs. Toroid Magnetics

Fall 2026 · In progress · Report coming soon

High-frequency magnetics course project to design, build and characterize a resonant inductive wireless power transfer (WPT) system that lights an LED circuit on a toy car as it rolls over a transmitter coil. The preliminary targets are 100 kHz operation, 20 W delivered (12 V square-wave drive, 5 V / 4 A rectified output) and at least 70% efficiency. I am sizing the magnetics analytically (turns, wire gauge, gap, core and copper loss) for two core families, a TDK P22/13 pot core and toroidal cores, and comparing how core geometry affects coupling, losses and efficiency. Next steps are Maxwell finite-element simulation, winding the coils, tuning the series resonant capacitors and the rectifier, and measuring delivered power and efficiency versus gap and misalignment against a coupled-coil circuit model.

250 MS/s 8-bit Current-Steering DAC in 0.18 µm CMOS

Design of a high-speed digital-to-analog converter targeting wireline transmitter applications, delivering ≥1.5 Vpp,diff into a 100 Ω differential load with SFDR exceeding 66 dB at the TT corner. The architecture uses a 3+5 segmented current-steering topology with thermometer-encoded MSBs, per-cell 6-bit trim DACs for ±10% mismatch correction, and retiming latches reducing inter-switch skew to ~1–2 ps. DNL remains within ±2×10−4 LSB across all three process corners.

Fully Differential Switched-Capacitor Amplifier in 0.18 µm CMOS

Design of a fully differential switched-capacitor sample-and-hold amplifier with 2× gain, intended as the residue amplification stage in a 7-bit 16 MHz pipelined ADC. The core is a folded-cascode OTA achieving 56.64 dB DC gain, 71° phase margin, 33.1 dB gain margin, and 167 MHz closed-loop bandwidth, with a settling error of 0.09% and total power consumption of 486.72 µW. A switched-capacitor CMFB circuit stabilizes the output common-mode at 0.9 V, achieving 333.4 dB CMRR.

2-Stage Low-Noise Amplifier for Starlink Ku-Band (11.4–12.7 GHz)

Design of a two-stage GaAs FET LNA on Duroid 5880 substrate targeting the Starlink customer terminal downlink band. The NE3210S01 HEMT was selected for its 0.34 dB NFmin at 12 GHz. The first stage is noise-matched to minimize Friis cascade noise figure; the second stage is gain-matched using Smith chart techniques. The final design achieves S21 > 23 dB, NF < 0.9 dB, S11 < −12 dB, and S22 < −14 dB across the band, with unconditional stability (µ > 1) from 0.1–20 GHz, IIP3 = 1 dBm, and 100 mW DC power.

Ultrafast Nonlinear Acoustics in Free-standing Membranes at the Nanoscale

This paper explores the properties of nonlinear acoustic strain wave propagation in solid crystals, integrating both experimental data analysis and numerical modeling. By introducing the Korteweg- De Vries (KdV) equation, we describe soliton propagation within certain nonlinear systems. The study simulates several physical scenarios, including the compression of a sequence of acoustic pulses, reflection at a free interface, and acoustic attenuation. These simulations provide insights into the complex behavior of nonlinear acoustic waves, which could have significant implications for the design and analysis of advanced materials and acoustic devices. This project was conducted at Laboratoire des Solides Irradiés (LSI) under the supervision of Dr. Vasily Temnov.

Rogue Waves Dynamics using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Rogue waves are rare, unpredictable surface waves that exceed twice the height of surrounding waves and can appear suddenly from unexpected directions. Unlike tsunamis, these localized phenomena—sometimes surpassing 30 meters—arise from nonlinear energy focusing and pose significant risks to even the largest ships. This study focuses on training PINNs to solve the self-focusing Non-Linear Schrödinger equation and model the balance between nonlinearity and viscosity. This internship took place at LadHyX under the supervision of Prof. Christophe Josserand in January-March 2025.

Construction and Characterization of a Lithium-Ion Coin Cell

Laboratory project (LAB103) carried out with Vlad-Ștefănuț Radu in September 2022. We built two coin-cell lithium-ion batteries (CR2025 and CR2032 formats) from scratch: graphite anode and LiMn2O4 cathode pastes bound with PTFE on stainless-steel mesh current collectors, a lithium triflate / propylene carbonate electrolyte, and a microfiber separator, sealed with a hydraulic press. After an unmeasured first cycle, we ran four charge–discharge cycles per cell, recording current, voltage and time to compute capacity and Coulombic efficiency (mean 70.6% and 58.5% for the two cells). We propagated measurement uncertainties to a 99% confidence level, compared our results with published values for commercial cells, and analyzed the likely causes of the gap, such as residual moisture and variability in electrode preparation.
